# 3D Rig Core (stage 1) Implementation Plan

**Goal:** Put the humanoid skeleton on a real 3D core: every joint is a `Vector3` in rig space, limbs are solved by 3D two-bone IK with a pole vector, and the 2D keys the drawing code reads are the projection of those joints. Nothing visual changes.

**Architecture:**
- New `FkRig` (`addons/figure_kit/rig.gd`): pure math. `project`, `depth`, `ik3`, `reach3`, `plane_pole`.
- `FkSkeleton.solve` builds a `p3` dictionary (joint → `Vector3`), solves legs and unsteered arms with `ik3`, then derives every existing joint key (`j.hand_n`, …) and `j.z[...]` from `p3` in one place. That place is where stage 2 adds the camera yaw.
- Consumers (`figure.gd`, `weapons.gd`, `armour.gd`) are untouched.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-29-3d-rig-side-view-design.md` (stage 1 of §6).

## Global Constraints

- Godot: `tools/godot` (`tools/setup_env.sh` once per cloud session). Always wrap it in `timeout`.
- Tests: `timeout 300 tools/godot --headless --path . -s tests/run_tests.gd`. Baseline: 148 tests, 0 failures.
- `addons/figure_kit/` must never name a game class, even in comments (`tests/test_fk_boundary.gd`).
- View-only change: no sim, no balance, nothing under `data/`.
- The 720-frame golden test (`test_default_look_matches_the_pre_rig_skeleton`) stays as is. It is the identity check.
- Commit style: `figure kit: …`.

**Where stage 1 is narrower than the spec (§3, §6), on purpose:**
1. **No `Frame` helper.** Pelvis and chest frames arrive with `pelvis_yaw` / `chest_yaw` in stage 2, where they are first needed.
2. **No `view` parameter on `solve`.** Stage 2 adds the camera yaw and plumbs `spec.view`. Until then `p3` is in rig space and the keys are its projection at yaw 0. Half-supporting a yaw here would leave `dir` and the boots unprojected.
3. **`key()` and `STANCES` are untouched.** `Vector3` hand targets and the `pole` channel are added in stage 4, with the first family that needs a lateral target. Every limb is still planar in its own shoulder or hip plane, so the pole is the in-plane one the old `bend` rule implies.
4. **The steered bow elbow (`el`, `_fit`) stays.** It retires in stage 3.
5. **No animation-page republish.** Nothing visual changes, so the evidence is the before/after sheets being byte-identical.

## Review Focus

1. **Identity.** The golden test (720 frames, 1e-3), plus every unit sheet (3 roles × 3 races, plus siege) byte-identical before and after.
2. **`ik3` equals `ik`** for in-plane poles, and the elbow moves continuously as the pole turns around the axis.
3. **Every bone keeps its exact 3D length in `p3`**, including the neck, head, toe and the steered bow elbow.
4. **Speed.** `solve()` stays within 2× of its baseline (about 60 µs per call on the cloud container, fixed 20000-call loop).

### Task 1: `FkRig` (nothing visual changes)

**Files:**
- Create: `addons/figure_kit/rig.gd`
- Test: `tests/test_fk_rig.gd` (new)

**Interfaces:**
- `FkRig.project(p: Vector3, view := {}) -> Vector2`: `(x·cos yaw − z·sin yaw, y)`; yaw 0 is `(x, y)`.
- `FkRig.depth(p: Vector3, view := {}) -> float`: `z·cos yaw + x·sin yaw`; yaw 0 is exactly `z`.
- `FkRig.at(p: Vector2, z := 0.0) -> Vector3`.
- `FkRig.reach3(root, target, l1, l2) -> Vector3`, `FkRig.ik3(root, target, l1, l2, pole) -> Vector3`.
- `FkRig.plane_pole(root, target, bend) -> Vector3`: the pole that reproduces `FkSkeleton.ik`'s fold side (`bend` = ±1, 0 = on the line) for a limb in a plane `z = const`.

**Tests:**
- [ ] `project` at yaw 0 is `(x, y)` and `depth` is `z`. At yaw 20° a near-side point (z > 0) shifts toward −x, and `(screen.x, depth)` is a rotation (lengths preserved).
- [ ] Mirroring: for any point and yaw, `project` of the point turned 180° about the vertical (`(−x, y, −z)`) is the negative screen x of the original, and its depth is negated.
- [ ] `ik3` with `plane_pole` equals `FkSkeleton.ik` for both bend signs over a grid of roots, targets and bone lengths (≤ 1e-3).
- [ ] `ik3` keeps both bone lengths (≤ 0.02) for targets at any z offset, with a pole out of the plane.
- [ ] Continuity: turning the pole a full circle around the root→target axis in 1° steps moves the joint at most `l1 · sin(a) · 1°` (plus a small epsilon) per step, and it stays at a constant distance from the axis.
- [ ] A pole parallel to the axis, or zero, puts the joint on the line (`root + axis · l1`) instead of returning NaN.
- [ ] `reach3` returns an in-reach target unchanged, and clamps a far one to full extension along the line.
